Theoretical Computer Science and Information Mathematics / LESSON 02

Information Content and Surprise

Goal for this lesson: explain "Information Content and Surprise" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Logic, combinatorics, and probability. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The self-information of an event of probability p is −log₂p, measured in bits. A rare event, when it occurs, delivers more information; entropy is the probability-weighted average of self-information.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Each outcome of a fair coin carries 1 bit of information, so the entropy is 1. A certain outcome has probability 1 and self-information 0. A binary random variable has maximum entropy at p=1/2; the more it favors one outcome, the more predictable it is.

Pause and Try It

Among four equally likely outcomes, how many bits of information does observing one outcome carry?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

−log₂(1/4)=2 bits. This corresponds to the two ideal binary choices needed to distinguish four outcomes.
