Theoretical Computer Science and Information Mathematics / LESSON 04

Computability and P/NP

Goal for this lesson: explain "Computability and P/NP" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Logic, combinatorics, and probability. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Computable means that an algorithm exists which always halts and returns an answer; by the diagonal argument, the halting problem is not computable. P is the class of problems solvable in polynomial time, and NP is the class for which a proposed solution can be verified in polynomial time; P⊆NP is clear, but whether the two are equal is an open Millennium Problem. NP-complete problems (such as SAT) are the hardest members of NP: every NP problem can be reduced to them in polynomial time.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Whether a Sudoku puzzle has a solution lies in NP: a filled-in grid can be checked quickly, while whether an algorithm guaranteed to solve it quickly exists is unknown. By the Cook–Levin theorem, SAT became the first NP-complete problem.

Pause and Try It

Why does "fast to verify" not imply "fast to solve"?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Verifying only checks a given answer, while solving must find one inside an exponentially large space of candidates — as with a jigsaw puzzle: checking an assembled puzzle is easy, but assembling one from a jumbled pile is hard. Unless P=NP, this asymmetry is essential, and certain cryptographic constructions rely on precisely it.
