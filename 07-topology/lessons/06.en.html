Topology / LESSON 06

The Fundamental Group: Counting Holes with Loops

Goal for this lesson: explain "The Fundamental Group" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: sets, maps; advanced topics require analysis and algebra. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A loop based at x₀ is a continuous map from [0,1] into the space with both endpoints x₀. Concatenate loops and identify them by homotopies that keep both endpoints fixed at x₀ throughout. The classes form π₁(X,x₀); fixing the basepoint makes concatenation well-defined on classes. The identity is the constant-loop class and inverses traverse loops backward. The circle has fundamental group Z, while the plane has trivial fundamental group. This encodes ways of winding around obstacles algebraically.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The plane with the origin removed: a loop winding twice around the origin cannot be continuously deformed into one winding three times (the deformation would have to sweep across the hole), and π₁(ℝ²∖{0})=Z. Homeomorphisms preserve the fundamental group: the line and the circle are not homeomorphic (one has trivial π₁, the other Z) — "rubber-sheet geometry" too has invariants you can count.

Pause and Try It

What generates the fundamental group of the figure eight (two circles glued at one point)? Why is the "hole structure" of the plane with two points removed said to be more complicated than with one point removed?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The generators go around the left and right circles. The fundamental group is the free group F₂≅Z∗Z; with the basepoint fixed, left-then-right and right-then-left are not homotopic. Removing one or two points from the plane gives Z or F₂, respectively. The group records winding order, not the precise geometric placement of holes, which can be changed by a homeomorphism.
