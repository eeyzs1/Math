Topology / LESSON 03

Connectedness: A Space That Cannot Be Split into Two Pieces

Goal for this lesson: explain "Connectedness" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: sets, maps; advanced topics require analysis and algebra. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Connected in the topological sense means the space cannot be written as a union of two disjoint, nonempty, relatively open sets. Among the real numbers, the intervals are the connected sets. This is more precise than "looks connected on the graph".

A Derivation, Step by Step

The set (0,1)∪(2,3) splits outright into these two nonempty open pieces and is therefore disconnected. A continuous map sends a connected space to a connected set; this is the backdrop of the intermediate value phenomenon.

Pause and Try It

A continuous function on an interval takes the values −1 and 1. Can it skip 0?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

No. The Intermediate Value Theorem guarantees that it takes the value 0 at some point in between. If the domain is not an interval, or the function is not continuous, the conclusion can fail.
