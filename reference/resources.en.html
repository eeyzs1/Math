Textbook Sources and How to Use Them

Retrieval date: 2026-11-01 (the advanced-direction addenda were verified on 2026-11-07). The items below are open textbooks and courses published directly by their authors or universities; their texts are not copied. The Chinese tutorial is an independently written introductory exposition, mirrored here in English. When reading the original material, look up chapters by topic — there is no need to grind through a graduate course from its first chapter.

Teaching Method

  • teach SKILL.md: read in full; its designs for short lessons, immediate feedback, retrieval, and spaced review were adapted. Found via find-skills; skills.sh showed roughly 896k installs on the page at the time and the GitHub API showed roughly 308k stars for the repository at the time — the numbers are retrieval snapshots and cannot replace a review of the textbook content. Not installed globally.
  • MSC2020: the scope reference for the research classification; the study groupings are this project's own.

Foundational Textbooks

  • OpenStax Precalculus 2e: functions, equations, trigonometry, analytic geometry; corresponds to 00, the elementary parts of 02, and the start of 05.
  • Oscar Levin, Discrete Mathematics: logic, proof, counting, graphs; corresponds to 01, the beginnings of 03, 08, and the algorithm preparation for 14.
  • Jim Hefferon, Linear Algebra: vector spaces, matrices, determinants; corresponds to 04. The original book's full course has additional prerequisites; the two lessons here need only elementary algebra.
  • OpenStax Calculus Volume 1: limits, derivatives, and integrals; corresponds to 06 and the foundations of 12 and 13.
  • OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e: probability, describing data, sampling, and inference; corresponds to 09 and 10.

Advanced Directions (not beginner reading)

  • MIT 18.703 Modern Algebra: the group–ring–field direction of 02.
  • MIT 18.785 Number Theory I: the algebraic number theory direction of 03; a graduate course.
  • MIT 18.901 Introduction to Topology: continuity, connectedness, compactness in 07.
  • MIT 18.03SC Differential Equations: the ordinary differential equations of 11, plus background for the oscillations of 15 and the growth models of 16.
  • MIT 18.303 Linear Partial Differential Equations: the PDE directions of 11 and 15.
  • MIT 18.335J Numerical Methods: computational error and stability in 12; graduate level.
  • MIT 6.003 Signals and Systems: signals, systems, and transform background, corresponding to the applications of 14 and 15.

The number-systems and axioms lessons of 17 serve as cross-chapter literacy guides; this edition does not include a chronology with original-source scholarship. Full convex optimization for 13, complete information theory and coding for 14, and the financial mathematics of 16 still need dedicated advanced bibliographies — the foundational textbooks above do not replace sources for those advanced areas.

Classification Data Attribution

assets/msc2020.csv is the original MSC2020 data, jointly prepared by Mathematical Reviews and zbMATH; the source site marks it CC BY-NC-SA. The original file keeps its original encoding; this project's Chinese renderings are simplified study translations and pedagogical groupings (this English edition simply retains the official English names). The classification data and its adaptations follow the attribution, non-commercial, and share-alike requirements.
