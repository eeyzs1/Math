01 · How a Secant Approaches the Tangent

The curve y=x², the fixed point (1,1), and a second point (1+h,(1+h)²). Predict: as h approaches 0, what does the secant slope approach?

The difference quotient is 2+h, so the limit is 2. The slider cannot reach 0, because the difference quotient is undefined at h=0 — the derivative is a limit. Blue is the curve, orange the secant, and the dashed line the tangent.

02 · How the Determinant Scales Area

The map A=diag(a,1). Predict: what happens when a=0, or when a is negative?

The dashed outline is the original square. The shaded rectangle has area |a| and oriented area a. At a=0 the square collapses onto a line; a negative a flips it to the other side. This demo shows a diagonal matrix only — general matrices do more than scale.

03 · How the Binomial Distribution Changes Shape

Ten independent coin flips, each landing heads with probability p. The bars show the theoretical probability of exactly k heads — not one random sample. Predict: as p moves from 0.5 to 0.9, which way does the peak shift?

P(X=k)=C(10,k)pᵏ(1−p)¹⁰⁻ᵏ. Expectation =10p, variance =10p(1−p). Equal expectations do not mean equal spread. At p=0 and p=1 the distribution concentrates on a single outcome.

04 · The Step Size Can Change the System's Behavior

For y′=−2y, y(0)=1, run Euler's method up to t=10: the next value is (1−2h)y. Predict: at h=1, does the numerical solution still decay?

Blue is the exact solution, orange the numerical one. For 0<h<1 the amplitude decays; at h=1 it oscillates without decay; for h>1 it diverges. The vertical axis auto-scales with the data, so read the numbers above as well.

05 · How Sample Means Become Normal

Each group flips a fair coin n times and records the proportion of heads; repeat for 2000 groups and draw a histogram of the means. Predict: how do the shapes for n=1 and n=50 differ?

The gray dashed curve is the theoretical normal density N(0.5, 0.25/n). As n grows the means concentrate at 0.5 while the spread shrinks like 1/√n — an experimental picture of the central limit theorem.

06 · Newton's Method Depends on the Initial Guess

Solve f(x)=x³−2x+2=0: from x₀ draw the tangent; its zero becomes the next iterate. Predict: what happens at x₀=0? And at x₀=−2?

The orange dashes are the successive tangents. Starting near 0, the iteration cycles between 0 and 1; near the real root (about −1.77) it converges quickly — a good starting point is part of Newton's method.

07 · Circle to Ellipse: the Eigenvalues Are the Semi-Axes

A symmetric matrix A=[[a,b],[b,d]] acts on the unit circle. Predict: what shape is the image? What determines the semi-axis directions? What happens when b=0?

The gray dashed circle is the unit circle; the green ellipse is its image; the orange segments are the two eigen-directions, each with length equal to the corresponding eigenvalue. Semi-axes =|λ|, directions = eigenvectors; |det| is the area scale factor. At b=0 the ellipse straightens out and its axes lie along the coordinate axes.
