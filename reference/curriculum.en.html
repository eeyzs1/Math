From Foundations to Research Training: Curriculum Overview

This is course design, not a claim that "all lessons are written". The 139 introductory lessons are ready to read; the advanced units on this page are not yet written. The 6,000-plus records in the classification index are not 6,000 unrelated courses either.

How "Mastery" Is Understood Here

There is no recognized "graduation line for masters of mathematics". This project sets the checkable upper bound at research preparation and initial research training in one direction: reading papers, filling in proofs, reproducing results, posing small questions, and accepting feedback. The branches of mathematics span widely; one usually builds a broad foundation and then goes deep in a few directions.

Promotion is based on evidence from work and problem-solving, not on browsing progress. Finishing a lesson does not automatically mean mastery, and finishing this overview does not mean finishing a textbook.

Completion Criteria for Authored Lessons

Each topic should have: explicit prerequisites and goals, conceptual intuition, precise definitions, theorem hypotheses, the necessary proofs, at least one fully worked example, basic and transfer exercises, hints and complete solutions, common mistakes, and credible sources. Topics that suit visualization additionally get manipulable figures, with a note that figures do not replace proofs.
