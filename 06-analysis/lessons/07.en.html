Series: Convergence and Divergence

Goal for this lesson: explain "Series" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: functions, algebra, coordinates. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A series is defined to converge when its sequence of partial sums converges. The geometric series Σrⁿ converges if and only if |r|<1, with sum 1/(1−r). The harmonic series Σ1/n diverges: for the terms to tend to zero is not enough. If the terms do not tend to zero, the series must diverge (a necessary condition).

A Derivation, Step by Step

For Σ(1/2)ⁿ summed from n=0, the partial sums are 2−(1/2)ⁿ, which tend to 2. Group the harmonic series as (1/3+1/4), (1/5+…+1/8), …; each group exceeds 1/2, so the total diverges.

Pause and Try It

Does Σ1/n² converge? What does the contrast with the harmonic series show?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

It converges: group the terms from 2ᵏ to 2ᵏ⁺¹−1. Within a group every n≥2ᵏ, so each term is at most 1/4ᵏ; a group contains at most 2ᵏ terms, so its sum is at most 2ᵏ×1/4ᵏ=1/2ᵏ, and Σ1/2ᵏ converges. Squaring makes the tail decay fast enough; what decides convergence is the rate of decay, not monotone decrease by itself.
