The Derivative as a Local Rate of Change

Goal for this lesson: explain "The Derivative as a Local Rate of Change" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: functions, algebra, coordinates. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The average rate of change is Δy/Δx. Fix a starting point x and let the increment h approach 0; if the limit of the quotient exists, it is the derivative f′(x). It is the slope of the local linear approximation to the curve.

A Derivation, Step by Step

For f(x)=x², [f(x+h)−f(x)]/h=2x+h (h≠0). Letting h approach 0 gives f′(x)=2x. At x=1 the slope is 2, not the function value 1.

Pause and Try It

Without appealing to a ready-made formula, find the derivative of f(x)=3x+2 at an arbitrary x.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The difference quotient is [3(x+h)+2−(3x+2)]/h=3, and the limit is 3. The function value changes from place to place, yet the slope stays constant.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
