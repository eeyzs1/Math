Integration Accumulates Local Quantities

Goal for this lesson: explain "Integration Accumulates Local Quantities" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: functions, algebra, coordinates. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The definite integral can be understood as the limit of a sum of signed areas of many thin rectangles. For a continuous function, if F′=f, then the integral from a to b is F(b)−F(a).

A Derivation, Step by Step

∫₀² x dx=[x²/2]₀²=2, which is also the area of the triangle with base 2 and height 2. Where the curve lies below the horizontal axis, the definite integral counts negative values.

Pause and Try It

What is ∫₋₁¹ x dx? Does it equal the total area enclosed by the curve and the horizontal axis?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The definite integral is 0, because the contributions of the left and right sides cancel. The total geometric area is 1; computing it requires integrating |x|. Area intuition must distinguish the signed from the unsigned case.
