Limits: What It Means to Approach

Goal for this lesson: explain "Limits" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: functions, algebra, coordinates. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The meaning of lim(x→a) f(x)=L: whenever x is sufficiently close to a (it need not equal a), f(x) can be made arbitrarily close to L. The epsilon–delta definition turns this into a checkable proposition: for every ε>0 one can find a δ>0 such that 0<|x−a|<δ implies |f(x)−L|<ε. A function is continuous exactly when the limiting value equals the function value.

A Derivation, Step by Step

f(x)=(x²−1)/(x−1) is undefined at x=1, but for x≠1 we have f(x)=x+1, so the limit is 2. A limit describes the behavior during the approach; it does not depend on whether the point is defined or on what value is taken there.

Pause and Try It

What is the key step in verifying lim(x→3) x²=9 with an epsilon–delta argument?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Control |x²−9|=|x−3||x+3|. First restrict |x−3|<1, which forces |x+3|<7; then take δ=min(1,ε/7), and |x²−9|<7δ≤ε follows. Boxing x in first and then estimating is the standard routine.
