Integration Techniques: Substitution and Integration by Parts

Goal for this lesson: explain "Integration Techniques" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: functions, algebra, coordinates. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Substitution is the chain rule in reverse: ∫f(g(x))g′(x)dx=F(g(x))+C; integration by parts is the product rule in reverse: ∫u dv=uv−∫v du. Mnemonic for choosing u: among logarithm, inverse trigonometric, power, trigonometric, exponential, whichever sits further to the left becomes u. These techniques do not change the definition of the integral; they merely turn "no antiderivative to be found" into "one can be found".

A Derivation, Step by Step

∫te^t dt: take u=t and dv=e^tdt, obtaining te^t−∫e^tdt=(t−1)e^t+C — the key step in solving y′+y=t by the integrating-factor method in 11 Differential Equations. ∫x·cos(x²)dx=(1/2)sin(x²)+C: substitute w=x², dw=2x dx.
