Euler's Method Is a Local Straight-Line Prediction

Goal for this lesson: explain "Euler's Method Is a Local Straight-Line Prediction" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra and calculus. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

To solve y′=f(t,y), step forward via yₙ₊₁=yₙ+h f(tₙ,yₙ). This is a tangent-line prediction; both the step size and stability affect the result.

A Derivation, Step by Step

For y′=y, y(0)=1 with step size 0.5, two steps give 1.5 and 2.25; the exact value is y(1)=e≈2.718. Local estimates accumulate error.

Pause and Try It

For y′=−2y with h=1 and h=0.25, what are the one-step factors? What does this show?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The factors are −1 and 0.5. The former produces non-decaying oscillation, the latter decays. The exact solution always decays exponentially, so too large a step can change the qualitative behavior.
