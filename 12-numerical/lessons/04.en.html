Newton's Method

Goal for this lesson: explain "Newton's Method" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra and calculus. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

To solve f(x)=0: at xₙ approximate f by its tangent; the tangent's zero becomes the next iterate: xₙ₊₁=xₙ−f(xₙ)/f′(xₙ). Near a simple root it converges quadratically (the number of correct digits roughly doubles per step), but it depends on a good initial guess, and it fails where the derivative is near zero or the iterates oscillate back and forth.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Computing √2 means solving x²−2=0: xₙ₊₁=(xₙ+2/xₙ)/2. Starting from 1 gives 1.5, 1.4167, 1.41422 — two steps reach the accuracy bisection needs a dozen steps for.

Pause and Try It

What happens when iterating f(x)=x³−2x+2 from x₀=0? What does this show?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

With f′=3x²−2: x₁=0−2/(−2)=1, then x₂=1−1/1=0 — the iteration cycles between 0 and 1 forever. Newton's method comes with only a local convergence guarantee; globally it can oscillate without advancing. In practice, screen the initial guess, or mix with bisection as a safety net.
