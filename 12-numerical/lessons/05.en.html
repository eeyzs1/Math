Numerical Integration: Trapezoidal and Simpson's Rules

Goal for this lesson: explain "Numerical Integration" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra and calculus. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The trapezoidal rule treats f as a straight line: ∫≈(b−a)(f(a)+f(b))/2, with composite error O(h²). Simpson's rule fits a parabola through three points: (b−a)/6·[f(a)+4f(midpoint)+f(b)], with composite error O(h⁴) — at the same number of nodes, the accuracy jumps by an order of magnitude. These composite error orders require suitably bounded second and fourth derivatives, respectively, on a fixed integration interval. Composite Simpson quadrature normally also requires an even number of equal subintervals.

A Derivation, Step by Step

∫₀¹1/(1+x)dx=ln2≈0.6931. Trapezoidal: (1+0.5)/2=0.75, an error of about 8%. Simpson: (1/6)(1+4×(2/3)+0.5)≈0.6944, an error of 0.2%.

Pause and Try It

Is the trapezoidal rule exact for linear functions? Is Simpson's rule exact for cubics?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The trapezoid rule is exact for polynomials of degree at most 1, so its algebraic degree of precision is 1. Simpson's rule is exact through degree 3 and has degree of precision 3; its panel error involves a fourth derivative. Algebraic precision differs from composite convergence order: the respective errors are O(h²) and O(h⁴), under suitable smoothness assumptions.
