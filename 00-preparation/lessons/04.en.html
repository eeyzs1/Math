Equations and Inequalities: From Inverse Operations to the Number Line

Goal for this lesson: explain "Equations and Inequalities" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations; beginners can start here. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Solving an equation means finding the inputs that make the equality hold: applying the same reversible operation to both sides leaves the solution set unchanged; moving a term across is shorthand for 'add the same quantity to both sides', and clearing denominators is 'multiply both sides'. Inequalities work the same way, except that multiplying or dividing by a negative number must flip the direction. The number line pictures the solution set: x>3 is a ray, and 2≤x<5 is a segment.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Solve 3x+4=13: subtract 4 from both sides to get 3x=9, then divide by 3 to get x=3 — every step is reversible. Solve −2x>6: dividing by −2 gives x<−3, with the direction flipped — multiplying by a negative number swaps 'larger' and 'smaller'.

Pause and Try It

Solve 5−2x≥1, sketch the solution set on the number line, and point out which step reverses the inequality.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

−2x≥−4; dividing both sides by −2 flips the direction and gives x≤2 — the interval (−∞,2] on the number line. The flip occurs because −2x≥−4 is equivalent to 2x≤4. Checking each step with 'is this transformation reversible, and did the direction change?' keeps you from losing solutions in equations and inequalities alike.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
