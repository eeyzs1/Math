Fractions: Another Name for Division

Goal for this lesson: explain "Fractions" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations; beginners can start here. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The fraction a/b denotes both 'a of b equal parts of 1' and the division a÷b. Equality is tested by cross-multiplication: a/b=c/d if and only if ad=bc. Addition and subtraction require a common denominator (converting to the same scale); multiplication and division do not: multiply numerators together and denominators together, and divide by multiplying by the reciprocal. Denominators b and d must be nonzero; dividing by a fraction also requires its numerator to be nonzero.

A Derivation, Step by Step

For 2/3+1/4, convert to twelfths: 8/12+3/12=11/12. And 2/3×3/4=6/12=1/2 by direct multiplication. Dividing by 3/4 asks 'how many servings of 3/4 fit inside?', which equals multiplying by 4/3.

Pause and Try It

Compute 3/5÷2/7, and explain why dividing by a fraction equals multiplying by its reciprocal.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

3/5×7/2=21/10. Let x=(3/5)÷(2/7); then (2/7)x=3/5, and multiplying both sides by 7/2 gives x=3/5×7/2. The reciprocal rule comes from 'division is the inverse of multiplication'.
