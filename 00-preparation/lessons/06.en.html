Powers and the Laws of Exponents

Goal for this lesson: explain "Powers and the Laws of Exponents" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations; beginners can start here. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

aⁿ is n copies of a multiplied together; the law aᵐ·aⁿ=aᵐ⁺ⁿ is a direct consequence of counting factors, not a formula to memorize. The zero exponent a⁰=1 keeps the law valid at m=0; negative exponents a⁻ⁿ=1/aⁿ preserve the addition law; and the fractional exponent a^(1/n)=ⁿ√a is forced by requiring (a^(1/n))ⁿ=a^(n·1/n).

A Derivation, Step by Step

2³·2⁴=2⁷=128 and 2⁵÷2³=2². Also 16^(1/2)=4 because 4²=16, and 8^(2/3)=(8^(1/3))²=2²=4.

Pause and Try It

Use the laws of exponents to compute 2⁻²×2⁵ and 27^(2/3), naming the law used at each step.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

2⁻²×2⁵=2³=8 (multiplying powers of the same base adds the exponents; a negative exponent means a reciprocal). 27^(2/3)=(27^(1/3))²=3²=9 — take the cube root first, then square; the order is guaranteed by (aʳ)ˢ=aʳˢ.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
