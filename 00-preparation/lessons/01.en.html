Integers, Negative Numbers, and Order of Operations

Goal for this lesson: explain "Integers, Negative Numbers, and Order of Operations" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations; beginners can start here. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Among the four arithmetic operations, addition and subtraction share one precedence level and multiplication and division another; 'multiplication and division before addition and subtraction' is not an arbitrary convention — it is what makes expansions like a(b+c)=ab+ac work. Negative numbers complete subtraction so that it can always be performed: 3−5 can be worked out because −2 is introduced. Estimation starts with orders of magnitude: multiply the leading digits, add the digit counts; a result off by an order of magnitude is certainly wrong.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Estimate 397×42 as 400×40=16000; the exact value is 16674, about 4% off. An order-of-magnitude check is enough to catch errors such as copying 16674 as 1667. With negative numbers, fix the sign first and the size second: (−3)×(−4)=12 — two flips of direction cancel each other.

Pause and Try It

Without a calculator, estimate 0.5×3980÷19.9, and write down what each step replaces with an approximation.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

It is about 0.5×4000÷20=100. Replacing 3980 by 4000 and 19.9 by 20 pushes the two approximations in opposite directions, so the errors partly cancel. Estimation checks plausibility; it does not replace exact computation.
