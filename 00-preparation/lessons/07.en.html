Logarithms: The Inverse of Exponentiation

Goal for this lesson: explain "Logarithms" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations; beginners can start here. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

log_a(x) answers 'a raised to what power equals x?' — it is the inverse of the exponential function: aʸ=x ⇔ y=log_a x. The exponent laws immediately give log(ab)=log a+log b (products become sums) and log(aⁿ)=n log a (powers come out front), plus the change-of-base formula log_b x=log_a x/log_a b.

A Derivation, Step by Step

log₂8=3, and log₂(8×16)=log₂128=7=3+4, mirroring 2³·2⁴=2⁷. Quotients become differences the same way: log(a/b)=log a−log b.

Pause and Try It

Given log₁₀2≈0.301, estimate log₁₀5.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Since 5=10/2, log₁₀5=log₁₀10−log₁₀2=1−0.301≈0.699. Turning division into subtraction is a direct corollary of the product rule — no new table needed.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
