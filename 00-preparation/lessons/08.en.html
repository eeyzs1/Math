Trigonometric Functions and Radians

Goal for this lesson: explain "Trigonometric Functions and Radians" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations; beginners can start here. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Radians are defined through arc length: a central angle that cuts an arc of length θ on the unit circle measures θ radians, and a half circle is π. sin and cos are the coordinates (cosθ,sinθ) of a point on the unit circle, so periodicity comes for free; sin²θ+cos²θ=1 is exactly the unit-circle equation x²+y²=1.

A Derivation, Step by Step

At θ=π/3 the point has coordinates (1/2,√3/2); the side ratios of the 30-60-90 and 45-45-90 triangles can both be read off the unit circle.

Pause and Try It

Why does sin²θ+cos²θ=1 hold for every θ? What are sin and cos at θ=2π?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Because (cosθ,sinθ) always lies on the unit circle x²+y²=1; substituting gives the identity. At θ=2π a full turn returns to the starting point (1,0), so sin 2π=0 and cos 2π=1 — the period 2π has its origin in the circle itself.
