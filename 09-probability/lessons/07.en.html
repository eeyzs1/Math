The Law of Large Numbers and the Central Limit Theorem

Goal for this lesson: explain "The Law of Large Numbers and the Central Limit Theorem" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Fractions and sets; later lessons require analysis. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Here X₁,X₂,… are independent and identically distributed. If E|X₁|<∞ with mean μ, the law of large numbers gives convergence in probability of the sample mean to μ. If additionally 0<σ²=Var(X₁)<∞, the central limit theorem says (X₁+…+Xₙ−nμ)/(σ√n) converges in distribution to N(0,1). Dependence, infinite variance and σ=0 are outside this version of the theorem.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Die-roll averages tend to 3.5; for 100 rolls the fluctuation of the mean is on the order of σ/√n≈1.71/10. Averaging repeated measurements compresses random error: precision improves like √n.

Pause and Try It

A fair coin is flipped 10,000 times. Roughly how far from 0.5 does the heads proportion land?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The standard deviation of the proportion is √(0.25/10000)=0.005, so it usually falls within 0.5±0.01 (about ±2σ). The law of large numbers guarantees convergence; the central limit theorem supplies the 1/√n fluctuation scale — doubling the precision takes four times the data.
