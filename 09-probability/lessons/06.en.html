Probability Theory / LESSON 06

Joint Distributions and Covariance

Goal for this lesson: explain "Joint Distributions and Covariance" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Fractions and sets; later lessons require analysis. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The joint distribution P(X=x,Y=y) describes paired outcomes; the marginals are obtained by summing over rows and columns. Independence holds if and only if the joint equals the product of the marginals. The covariance Cov(X,Y)=E[(X−EX)(Y−EY)] measures linear co-variation, its sign giving the direction; Var(X±Y)=VarX+VarY±2Cov.

A Derivation, Step by Step

With two dice, let X be the first die and Y the total: Cov(X,Y)=Var(X)=35/12 — the sum contains X's share. If Y=7−X, then Cov=−Var(X), a perfectly negative linear relation.

Pause and Try It

What is Cov when X and Y are independent? Does Cov=0 imply independence?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Independence ⇒ E[XY]=EX·EY ⇒ Cov=0. The converse fails: let X take −1, 0, 1 with equal probabilities and Y=X²; then Cov=E[X³]−EX·EX²=0, yet Y is completely determined by X. Zero covariance rules out only linear relationships.
