Statistics / LESSON 05

Hypothesis Testing

Goal for this lesson: explain "Hypothesis Testing" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Probability and algebra; binomial coefficients: see 08. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

First state the null hypothesis H₀ (say, "no effect"), then compute, under H₀, the probability of a result at least this extreme (the p-value); if p is smaller than the significance level α fixed in advance, reject H₀. α is the Type I error rate; the probability of failing to reject when rejection is called for is the Type II error rate β, and the power of the test, 1−β, depends on the sample size and the true effect size. The p-value is not "the probability that H₀ is true", and it does not measure the size of an effect.

A Derivation, Step by Step

A fair coin lands heads 18 times in 20 tosses: under H₀: p=0.5, P(≥18)=(C(20,18)+C(20,19)+C(20,20))/2²⁰=(190+20+1)/1048576≈0.0002, so "fair" is rejected (here as a one-sided test for a bias toward heads; the two-sided p-value is doubled, and the conclusion is unchanged).

Pause and Try It

Is p=0.03 the same thing as "the null hypothesis has only a 3% chance of being true"?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

No. The p-value is "the probability of data this extreme if H₀ is true" — the conditioning points the other way; P(H₀|data) also depends on the prior and on the power of the test. A small p-value says that the data conflict with H₀; it does not directly give the probability of H₀.
