What an Average Can Hide

Goal for this lesson: explain "What an Average Can Hide" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Probability and algebra; binomial coefficients: see 08. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The mean is the sum divided by the count; the median is the middle position once the data are sorted. They answer different questions. To describe a dataset you should also look at its spread, its outliers, and the way the sample was drawn.

A Derivation, Step by Step

For the income data 1,2,3,4,90, the mean is 20 and the median is 3. Most entries lie far below the mean, which shows that a single measure of center is not enough to describe a distribution.

Pause and Try It

If 90 is changed to 900, what happens to the median and to the mean?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The median is still 3, while the mean becomes 182. The median is more robust to a single extreme value; that does not mean the mean should be abandoned in every setting.
