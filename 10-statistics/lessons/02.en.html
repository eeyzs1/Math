Statistics / LESSON 02

Variance and Standard Deviation: The Scale of Variability

Goal for this lesson: explain "Variance and Standard Deviation" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Probability and algebra; binomial coefficients: see 08. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The variance is the average of the squared deviations of the data from the mean, and the standard deviation is its square root, which carries the same units as the original data. Datasets with the same mean can have very different standard deviations; when the standard deviation is small, the data cluster near the mean. For a normal distribution, about 68% of the data fall within ±1 standard deviation of the mean; for unimodal, roughly symmetric data this is only a rough rule of thumb (normal distribution: see 09, Lesson 05).

A Derivation, Step by Step

For 1,2,3,4,90: the mean is 20, the squared deviations are 361+324+289+256+4900=6130, the variance is 1226, and the standard deviation is about 35. A single extreme value drives this measure of variability extremely high, in contrast with the robustness of the median.

Pause and Try It

Find the mean, variance, and standard deviation of 2,4,6,8.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The mean is 5; the deviations are −3,−1,1,3, the sum of squares is 20, the variance is 5, and the standard deviation is √5≈2.24. When the data are symmetric the deviations are symmetric too, and every point falls within ±2 standard deviations of the mean.
