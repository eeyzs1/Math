Point Estimation and Confidence Intervals

Goal for this lesson: explain "Point Estimation and Confidence Intervals" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Probability and algebra; binomial coefficients: see 08. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A statistic estimates a fixed unknown parameter; unbiasedness means its expectation equals that parameter. For independent identically distributed normal observations with known population standard deviation σ, X̄±1.96σ/√n has approximately 95% coverage. For nonnormal populations satisfying the central limit theorem conditions it is a large-sample approximation. Confidence describes coverage over repeated samples, not a 95% posterior probability for an already fixed μ.

A Derivation, Step by Step

With n=25, sample mean 10, and σ=5: the interval is 10±1.96×5/√25=10±1.96, that is, (8.04, 11.96).

Pause and Try It

What goes wrong if you plug in 1.96 directly when σ is unknown and the sample is small?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

With unknown σ, estimate variance by s²=Σ(Xᵢ−X̄)²/(n−1). For independent identically distributed normal observations, (X̄−μ)/(s/√n) has a t distribution with n−1 degrees of freedom, giving X̄±t_{0.975,n−1}s/√n. Using 1.96 in a small normal sample usually undercovers. For a small nonnormal sample, replacing the quantile by t alone does not guarantee 95% coverage.
