A Model Must State Its Assumptions

Goal for this lesson: explain "A Model Must State Its Assumptions" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; requires derivatives from 06 and basic differential equations from 11. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A model is a simplification that keeps the key mechanisms for a specific question. It should state its variables, units, parameters, assumptions, and scope of validity, and be checked against new data; a good fit does not mean a correct mechanism.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The population model N′=rN implicitly assumes a constant per-capita growth rate and unlimited resources. It may fit the short run, yet it predicts unbounded growth in the long run. With limited resources, consider the logistic model.

Pause and Try It

To predict a commute with 'distance = speed × time', which assumptions are needed?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The speed should be the average over the whole trip, or the speed should be assumed constant; one should also state whether waits and detours are included. Parameters should be estimated for the route and time of day in question, and the prediction error checked.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
