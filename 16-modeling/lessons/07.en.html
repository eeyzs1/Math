Feedback Loops: Positive, Negative, and Systems

Goal for this lesson: explain "Feedback Loops" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; requires derivatives from 06 and basic differential equations from 11. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Feedback sends output back to influence input. Negative feedback acts against a deviation; positive feedback reinforces it. Multiplying the signs around a loop identifies its feedback sign, not its stability. Excessive gain, delay or a discrete step can make negative feedback oscillate or diverge. Stability requires the actual equations: xₙ₊₁=−2xₙ reverses a deviation but doubles its magnitude each step.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Thermostat: temperature below the set point → heating on → temperature recovers → heating off (a negative loop, oscillating mildly around the set point). Bank run: a rumor → a few withdrawals → the queue lengthens → more panic (a positive loop, until collapse). Newton's law of cooling in 11 is the continuous version of negative feedback: the larger the temperature difference, the faster the heat loss.

Pause and Try It

Is market-price adjustment of supply and demand positive or negative feedback? Draw the loop and label the direction of deviation at each step.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Price above equilibrium can increase supply and reduce demand, creating a surplus that pushes price down: this is negative feedback by sign. Convergence depends on adjustment speed, supply and demand responses and delays; overcorrection can oscillate or diverge. Draw the signed loop, then analyze an explicit model's equilibria and stability. Counting negative signs alone is insufficient.
