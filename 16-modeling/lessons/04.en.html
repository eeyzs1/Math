Nondimensionalization: Fewer Parameters, More Essence

Goal for this lesson: explain "Nondimensionalization" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; requires derivatives from 06 and basic differential equations from 11. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Substituting variables built from the problem's intrinsic scales compresses the equation's parameters into a few dimensionless numbers. Three benefits: fewer parameters, experiments that can be scaled, and a clear view of which terms dominate. Dimensional analysis is the entry point (see Lesson 01 of 15); here we treat the equation itself.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Logistic N′=rN(1−N/K): set u=N/K and τ=rt, and the equation becomes u′=u(1−u). Of the three parameters (r, K, N₀) only the combination u₀=N₀/K remains — all logistic growth is one and the same curve, stretched and squeezed.

Pause and Try It

How many parameters remain after nondimensionalizing m·dv/dt=−mg, v(0)=v₀?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Take the velocity scale v₀ (requiring v₀≠0; for release from rest the scale must instead come from, e.g., the drop height) and the time scale v₀/g, and set u=v/v₀: the equation becomes du/dτ=−1 with u(0)=1. The mass m disappears entirely — the reason heavy objects fall at the same speed is revealed automatically.
