A Complete Case: Exponential or Logistic?

Goal for this lesson: explain "A Complete Case" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; requires derivatives from 06 and basic differential equations from 11. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Modeling runs the full pipeline: question (will growth stay exponential forever?) → variables and units → candidate models (N′=rN vs. N′=rN(1−N/K)) → fitting parameters to data → testing on new data → stating the scope of validity. The logistic model writes 'limited resources' directly into the equation, with K the carrying capacity.

A Derivation, Step by Step

While N is far below K, the logistic model is nearly exponential, and both fit the early data equally well — the first half alone cannot distinguish the mechanisms. Once the data approach K, the exponential model runs systematically high and loses. r is fixed by the early growth rate, K by the leveling-off level.

Pause and Try It

Both models fit the first half of the data. What does this show, and what else does model selection rest on?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

It shows the first half has poor identifiability: for N≪K the two models' predictions nearly coincide. Selection must also lean on the second half or on new data, and on whether the mechanism is plausible. The equilibrium and stability analysis of the logistic equation appears in Lesson 04 of 11. Choosing a model is choosing a mechanism — not choosing the prettier fit.
