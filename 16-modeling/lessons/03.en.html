Sensitivity: What If a Parameter Is a Little Off?

Goal for this lesson: explain "Sensitivity" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; requires derivatives from 06 and basic differential equations from 11. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Model outputs often depend on uncertain parameters. Sensitivity analysis fixes everything else, varies one parameter, and watches the output respond; it reveals which quantities deserve the most careful measurement.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The compound-interest model A=P(1+r)ⁿ (a constant rate per period): doubling P doubles A, while the effect of the interest rate compounds over n periods. Long-range extrapolation is especially sensitive to the growth-rate assumption.

Pause and Try It

With P=100 and n=2, what do r=0.1 and r=0.2 give? Can this be treated as a guaranteed return?
