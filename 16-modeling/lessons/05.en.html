Fitting, Extrapolation, and Overfitting

Goal for this lesson: explain "Fitting, Extrapolation, and Overfitting" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; requires derivatives from 06 and basic differential equations from 11. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Prediction inside the observed input range is interpolation in the broad predictive sense, while strict mathematical interpolation also requires passing through specified data points; regression allows residuals. Prediction outside the range is extrapolation and relies more heavily on mechanism assumptions. Overfitting means good training fit but poor generalization. Many parameters increase the risk, but parameters need not outnumber observations. Holdout validation, regularization and comparison with simpler models help diagnose it.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Two points with distinct x-coordinates determine one line but admit infinitely many quadratic or higher-degree polynomials through them. At n distinct nodes, exactly one polynomial of degree at most n−1 interpolates the values. This is a full-rank linear problem, not a theorem that arbitrary models with as many parameters as data points fit perfectly or uniquely. Zero training error alone proves neither a mechanism nor generalization.

Pause and Try It

What is the relation between 'the first half of the data cannot distinguish exponential from logistic' and overfitting?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Both can reflect insufficient information, but identifiability and overfitting differ. When N≪K, exponential and logistic predictions are close: this is a practical identifiability difficulty in the observed range, not necessarily strict structural nonidentifiability. Overfitting concerns poor generalization and can occur with fewer parameters than observations. Broader observations, independent validation and simpler comparisons help; training error alone is insufficient.
