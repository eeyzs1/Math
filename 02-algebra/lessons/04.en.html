Algebra and Structure / LESSON 04

Groups: Symmetry as an Operation

Goal for this lesson: explain "Groups" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Fractions, equations, and basic proofs. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A group consists of a set together with an operation, satisfying closure, associativity, the existence of an identity element, and inverses. It abstracts the idea of "perform one action, then perform another." Commutativity is not required of a group.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The rotations of an equilateral triangle about its center through 0°, 120°, and 240° form a group. A 120° rotation followed by a 240° rotation makes 360°, which is the identity action. Every rotation has another rotation that undoes it.

Pause and Try It

If we keep only 0° and 120°, do we still get a rotation group?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

No. Composing 120° with 120° yields 240°, which is not in the set, so closure fails. When studying an algebraic structure, first inspect the set together with its operation rather than merely counting elements.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
