Subgroups and Homomorphisms

Goal for this lesson: explain "Subgroups and Homomorphisms" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Fractions, equations, and basic proofs. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A subgroup is a subset that is a group in its own right (it contains the identity element and is closed under the operation and under inverses); for example, the even integers form a subgroup of the additive group of the integers. A homomorphism f:G→H preserves the operation, f(ab)=f(a)f(b); the kernel ker f is the set of all elements sent to the identity, and it measures how far f is from injective: the kernel is trivial if and only if f is injective.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The "mod 6" map Z→Z/6 is a homomorphism of additive groups, with kernel the multiples of 6. The identity det(AB)=det(A)det(B) says that det is a homomorphism between multiplicative groups (the determinant is defined in 04 Linear Algebra, Lesson 04; we will double-check this claim when we get there).

Pause and Try It

Why is the set of even integers a subgroup of (Z,+), and why is the set of odd integers not?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The even integers contain 0, even+even=even, and the negative of an even integer is still even — all the conditions are met. The odd integers are not closed: 1+1=2 is even. The subgroup test only requires a nonempty subset closed under the operation and under inverses; the identity element then comes along for free.
