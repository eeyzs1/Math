Complex Numbers: Taking the Square Root of −1

Goal for this lesson: explain "Complex Numbers" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Fractions, equations, and basic proofs. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Introduce i²=−1 and extend the number system to a+bi. Addition and subtraction work componentwise; for multiplication, expand and then substitute i²=−1. The conjugate is a−bi and the modulus is √(a²+b²); for division, multiply the numerator and denominator by the conjugate so that the denominator becomes real. When Δ<0, a quadratic equation with real coefficients has a pair of complex conjugate roots.

A Derivation, Step by Step

(1+2i)(3−i)=3−i+6i−2i²=5+5i. For division, multiply the numerator and denominator of (1+2i)÷(3−i) by (3+i): the denominator becomes 9+1=10, the numerator becomes (1+2i)(3+i)=1+7i, and the result is 1/10+7i/10.

Pause and Try It

Solve x²+1=0 and x²+2x+5=0.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

x²=−1 gives x=±i. For the second, completing the square gives (x+1)²=−4, so x=−1±2i. The two roots are conjugates of each other, illustrating that complex roots of real-coefficient equations occur in pairs.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
