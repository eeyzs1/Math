Why Manipulating Equations Requires Checking Conditions

Goal for this lesson: explain "Why Manipulating Equations Requires Checking Conditions" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Fractions, equations, and basic proofs. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Solving an equation means searching for the inputs that make the equality hold. Reversible manipulations preserve the solution set; irreversible ones may introduce or lose solutions. Before dividing by an expression, you must confirm that it is nonzero.

A Derivation, Step by Step

x²=x can be rewritten as x(x−1)=0, so x=0 or x=1. Dividing both sides by x outright leaves only x=1 and drops the zero solution.

Pause and Try It

Solve (x−2)(x+3)=0, and explain why it can be split into two equations.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

In the real numbers the product of two nonzero numbers is nonzero, so a product can be zero only if at least one factor is zero. The solutions are 2 and −3. This property does not extend unconditionally to arbitrary algebraic structures.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
