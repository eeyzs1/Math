Equivalence Relations and Quotient Sets

Goal for this lesson: explain "Equivalence Relations and Quotient Sets" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

An equivalence relation is a binary relation that is reflexive (a~a), symmetric (a~b⇒b~a), and transitive (a~b, b~c⇒a~c) — for instance 'congruent modulo n' or 'similar triangles'. It cuts the universe into pairwise disjoint equivalence classes; the collection of all classes forms the quotient set. A quotient is 'treating equivalent things as the same thing'.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The integers split into three classes modulo 3: [0], [1], and [2]; for example [2]={…,−4,−1,2,5,…}. The quotient set Z/3 has only three elements, and clock arithmetic is precisely it.

Pause and Try It

Is 'equal absolute value' an equivalence relation on the integers? What are its equivalence classes?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Yes: |a|=|a| is reflexive; |a|=|b|⇒|b|=|a| is symmetric; transitivity is clear. The classes have the form {n,−n} (with 0 alone in its own class). It groups together numbers that 'differ in sign but not in size'.
