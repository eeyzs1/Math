Mathematical Foundations and Proof / LESSON 03

Contrapositives and Proof by Contradiction

Goal for this lesson: explain "Contrapositives and Proof by Contradiction" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A statement P⇒Q and its contrapositive ¬Q⇒¬P always agree in truth value, and proving the contrapositive is often easier. Proof by contradiction assumes the conclusion is false, derives a conflict with something known or with an axiom, and concludes that the conclusion must be true; it is especially suited to negative conclusions such as 'there is no...' or 'irrational'.

A Derivation, Step by Step

√2 is irrational: if √2=p/q in lowest terms, squaring gives p²=2q², so p is even; writing p=2k and substituting yields q²=2k², so q is even as well — contradicting 'lowest terms'.

Pause and Try It

Rewrite 'if n² is even then n is even' as its contrapositive and prove it; explain why this route is smoother.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Contrapositive: if n is odd, then n² is odd. Let n=2k+1; then n²=4k²+4k+1=2(2k²+2k)+1, which is indeed odd. Counting factors of 2 inside n² directly is a detour — the contrapositive is a one-line computation.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
