Mathematical Foundations and Proof / LESSON 04

Induction Is a Row of Dominoes

Goal for this lesson: explain "Induction Is a Row of Dominoes" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Induction has two indispensable steps: the base case holds; and for every k, if case k holds, then case k+1 holds. It applies to statements indexed by the natural numbers.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Prove 1+…+n=n(n+1)/2. It holds at n=1. Assuming it holds for k, adding k+1 gives k(k+1)/2+(k+1)=(k+1)(k+2)/2 — exactly the next case.

Pause and Try It

Prove that the first n positive odd numbers sum to n².

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Base: 1=1². If the first k terms sum to k², then adding the (k+1)-st odd number 2k+1 gives (k+1)². Writing down only the result, without the induction hypothesis and the induction step, is not yet a complete inductive proof.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
