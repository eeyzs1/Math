Recursive Definitions and Recurrences

Goal for this lesson: explain "Recursive Definitions and Recurrences" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: The four arithmetic operations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Recursion defines each next term from the earlier ones, as in F₁=F₂=1, Fₙ=Fₙ₋₁+Fₙ₋₂. It pairs with induction: recursion defines objects, induction proves properties. To find a closed form, compute the first few terms, guess the pattern, then verify it by induction.

A Derivation, Step by Step

For a₁=2 and aₙ=2aₙ₋₁ the general term is aₙ=2ⁿ: it holds at a₁=2, and if aₖ=2ᵏ then aₖ₊₁=2·2ᵏ=2ᵏ⁺¹.

Pause and Try It

What is the closed form of a₁=1, aₙ=aₙ₋₁+2n−1? Guess first, then prove.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The first terms are 1, 4, 9, 16, suggesting aₙ=n². Induction: if aₙ₋₁=(n−1)², then aₙ=(n−1)²+2n−1=n². The recursion automatically verifies that 'sums of consecutive odd numbers are perfect squares'.
