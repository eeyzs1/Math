Extending Number Systems Repairs the Boundaries of the Operations

Goal for this lesson: explain "Extending Number Systems Repairs the Boundaries of the Operations" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Curiosity and basic algebra. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The natural numbers serve counting, but 3−5 is not among them; the integers accommodate subtraction and the rationals accommodate division by nonzero divisors; the reals complete the structure needed for limits; the complex numbers allow squares equal to −1.

A Derivation, Step by Step

x²=2 has no solution in the rationals but ±√2 in the reals. x²+1=0 has no solution in the reals but ±i in the complex numbers. Saying 'no solution' requires naming the number system.

Pause and Try It

What happens to x+3=1 in the natural numbers versus the integers?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

No solution in the natural numbers; in the integers the solution is −2. The rules of the operations and the set of solutions depend on the universe under discussion.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
