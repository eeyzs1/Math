History, Philosophy, and Mathematical Literacy / LESSON 06

Reading Theorems, Writing Proofs: Mathematical Communication

Goal for this lesson: explain "Reading Theorems, Writing Proofs" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Curiosity and basic algebra. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Reading a theorem in four steps: ① what are the hypotheses and the conclusion; ② where does the proof use each hypothesis; ③ drop a hypothesis and hunt for a counterexample; ④ produce an example of your own. Writing a proof runs in reverse: state the strategy first (contradiction/induction/construction), then give each step its reason, and close by returning to the conclusion. Educational research agrees: retelling to others and spaced redoing beat rereading by far — the 'closed-book recall' of this course was designed for exactly that.

A Derivation, Step by Step

For a continuous real function on a nonempty closed bounded interval, isolate the hypotheses. Without continuity, set f(0)=0 and f(x)=1/x for x>0 on [0,1]; it is unbounded. Without closedness, f(x)=x on (0,1) never attains its supremum 1. Without boundedness, f(x)=x on [0,∞) has no maximum. Removing one key hypothesis at a time identifies its role.

Pause and Try It

Rewrite 'the first n positive odd numbers sum to n²' from the induction lesson of 01 in the communication format 'strategy first, then steps'.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Strategy: induction — the base case lays the foundation and the step passes it along. Steps: ① n=1: 1=1²; ② assume the first k terms sum to k²; ③ add the (k+1)-st odd number 2k+1 to get (k+1)²; ④ this holds for all n, each step using only definitions and the induction hypothesis. Stating 'which hypothesis was used where' lets the reader check — checkability is what separates mathematical communication from persuasion.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
