Combinatorics and Graph Theory / LESSON 04

Recurrence Relations

Goal for this lesson: explain "Recurrence Relations" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The first-order geometric recurrence aₙ=raₙ₋₁ solves to aₙ=rⁿa₀. The Fibonacci type aₙ=aₙ₋₁+aₙ₋₂ yields a closed form via the characteristic equation t²=t+1 — the 'characteristic equation' is of the same origin as the matrix eigenvalues of Lesson 07 in 04, and it echoes the characteristic equations of the second-order ODEs in 11. Solving proceeds in three steps: compute the first terms, guess the form, verify by induction.

A Derivation, Step by Step

a₀=1, aₙ=3aₙ₋₁ gives aₙ=3ⁿ. For the Fibonacci numbers 1,1,2,3,5,8,… the successive ratios 1, 2, 1.5, 1.667, … tend to the golden ratio (1+√5)/2 — exactly the ratio of the two characteristic roots.

Pause and Try It

What are the first four terms of a₀=2, aₙ=2aₙ₋₁+n? What structure do you observe?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

a₁=5, a₂=12, a₃=27, a₄=58. The successive differences 3, 7, 15, 31 nearly double: the solution is dominated by the homogeneous part c·2ⁿ, with the +n term a small perturbation. The solution of a linear recurrence is the homogeneous solution plus a particular solution.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
