Combinatorics and Graph Theory / LESSON 03

The Handshaking Lemma

Goal for this lesson: explain "The Handshaking Lemma" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A graph consists of vertices and edges. In an undirected graph, the degree of a vertex is the number of edge-ends incident to it. Every edge contributes two endpoints, so the sum of all degrees equals twice the number of edges.

A Derivation, Step by Step

If a graph has degrees 3, 2, 2, 1 in turn, the degree sum is 8 and the number of edges must be 4. The number of odd-degree vertices must be even — otherwise the total would be odd.

Pause and Try It

Can an undirected graph have degree sequence 3,3,3,2?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

No. The degree sum is 11, which is not even. This is only a necessary condition; an even sum does not guarantee that an arbitrary degree sequence is realizable.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
