Combinatorics and Graph Theory / LESSON 08

Eulerian Circuits and Graph Coloring

Goal for this lesson: explain "Eulerian Circuits and Graph Coloring" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A finite connected undirected graph has an Euler circuit if and only if every vertex has even degree; the circuit uses each edge exactly once and returns to its start. For a loopless simple graph, adjacent vertices must receive different colors and χ(G) is the minimum number. A graph is bipartite if and only if it has no odd cycle, equivalently χ(G)≤2. Equality χ(G)=2 requires at least one edge; a nonempty edgeless graph needs only one color.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The seven bridges of Königsberg: the four landmasses have degrees 3, 3, 3, 5, all odd, so no Eulerian circuit exists — graph theory explains the failure of the one-stroke drawing in a single sentence. The triangle has χ=3: its three vertices are pairwise adjacent, so two colors do not suffice.

Pause and Try It

Prove that a graph with χ=2 contains no triangle; then exhibit a graph with χ=3 in which every vertex has even degree.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

If it contained a triangle, the three pairwise-adjacent vertices would force three colors, contradicting χ=2. The triangle has every vertex of degree 2 (even) while χ=3 — colorability and degree parity are two independent global properties.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
