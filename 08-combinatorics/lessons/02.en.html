Combinatorics and Graph Theory / LESSON 02

When Counting, First Ask Whether Order Matters

Goal for this lesson: explain "When Counting, First Ask Whether Order Matters" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Choosing k objects from n distinct ones without replacement: with order, there are n!/(n−k)! outcomes; without order, each group is over-counted k! times, so the binomial coefficient is C(n,k)=n!/[k!(n−k)!].

A Derivation, Step by Step

Choosing 2 of 5 people for two distinct roles gives 5×4=20 ways; choosing a 2-person team gives 20/2=10. The difference comes from the two orderings of the same pair.

Pause and Try It

How many 3-person teams can be formed from 6 people? And how many ways are there to appoint a captain, a deputy, and a recorder?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Teams: C(6,3)=20; three distinct roles: 6×5×4=120. Both assume each person is chosen at most once.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
