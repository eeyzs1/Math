Introduction to Generating Functions

Goal for this lesson: explain "Introduction to Generating Functions" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Encoding a sequence a₀,a₁,… into the formal power series A(x)=Σaₙxⁿ turns sequence operations into series operations: multiplying by x shifts right, and multiplying two series convolves them. The basic identity is 1/(1−x)=1+x+x²+…; the generating function of the Fibonacci numbers is x/(1−x−x²), whose partial-fraction expansion delivers the closed form and the golden ratio.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The all-ones sequence has generating function 1/(1−x); multiplying by x gives 0,1,1,1,…. In counting, 'multiply the stages' corresponds to multiplying generating functions and reading off a coefficient.

Pause and Try It

Use 1/(1−x)²=(Σxⁿ)(Σxⁿ) to explain why its coefficients are 1,2,3,4,…

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Product coefficients are convolutions: the coefficient of xᵏ equals Σ_{i+j=k}1×1=k+1, giving 1,2,3,…. Generating functions translate 'count by summing' into 'multiply and extract a coefficient' — the source of their power over recurrences and counting problems.
