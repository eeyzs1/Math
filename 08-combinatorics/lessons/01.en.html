Pigeonhole and Inclusion–Exclusion: The Two Workhorses of Counting

Goal for this lesson: explain "Pigeonhole and Inclusion–Exclusion" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Pigeonhole principle: put n+1 objects into n drawers and some drawer holds at least two objects — it delivers existence without a construction. The inclusion–exclusion principle handles overlapping counts: |A∪B|=|A|+|B|−|A∩B|; the overlap is added twice and must be subtracted back once, and with more sets the additions and subtractions alternate.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Among any 13 people, two share a zodiac sign (13 people, 12 signs). From 1 to 100, the multiples of 2 or 3 number 50+33−16=67: the multiples of 6 were counted twice and are subtracted back.

Pause and Try It

How many numbers from 1 to 100 are divisible by 2 or 5? Justify every step.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

50 are divisible by 2, 20 by 5, and 10 by 10, giving 50+20−10=60. The key to counting 'or' is to identify the full overlap (the multiples of 10) before subtracting.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
