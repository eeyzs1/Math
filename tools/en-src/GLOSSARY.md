# Translation Standard: Chinese → English (Mathematics Course)

This glossary governs all English translations for this repository. Follow it exactly so
the 139 lessons, concept checklists, and docs read as one coherent, professionally
translated course. When a term is missing here, use the standard rendering from mainstream
English textbooks (e.g., Stewart for calculus, Axley/Hefferon for linear algebra, Tao for
analysis, Ross for probability), and report your choice in your final summary.

## Global rules

1. **Register**: professional mathematical English. Full sentences, "if and only if" in prose
   (never "iff" in running text). American spelling (behavior, labeled, center).
2. **Preserve all mathematical content exactly**: formulas, numbers, equations, units,
   symbols, superscripts/subscripts (x², 2ᵏ, Fₙ, λ, √2, ≡, ⊆, ∞). Never convert xⁿ to x^n.
   Keep variable names and matrices verbatim: [[0,−1],[1,0]] stays as-is.
3. **Punctuation**: convert Chinese punctuation to English equivalents. （）→ ( ),
   ；→ ; , ：→ : , 。→ . , ，→ , , “ ” → " ". No full-width characters may remain.
4. **Translate meaning, not words**: the source is dense; aim for natural mathematical
   English a competent textbook editor would accept. Rephrase rather than transliterate.
5. **Named theorems keep standard English names**: Pythagorean theorem, quadratic formula,
   Fermat's Little Theorem, Bézout identity, Chinese Remainder Theorem, law of quadratic
   reciprocity, Euler's criterion, Lagrange's theorem, rank–nullity theorem, Mean Value
   Theorem, Intermediate Value Theorem, Extreme Value Theorem, Fundamental Theorem of
   Calculus, Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic, Heine–Borel theorem, binomial theorem,
   law of sines, law of cosines, inscribed angle theorem, handshaking lemma, pigeonhole
   principle, inclusion–exclusion principle, law of large numbers, central limit theorem,
   Bayes' rule, law of total probability, spectral theorem, Cook–Levin theorem,
   Gödel's incompleteness theorems, Cantor's diagonal argument.
6. **People/places stay in Latin form**: Euler, Bézout, Cantor, Weierstrass, Cauchy,
   Kelvin, Newton, Leibniz, Berkeley, Klein, Galois, Sylow, Runge, Hamming, Nash,
   Markov, Pólya, Weyl, Gödel, Brouwer, Hilbert, Frege, Russell, d'Alembert, Coulomb,
   Galileo, Lorentz, Maxwell, Navier–Stokes, Dirichlet, Lebesgue, Riemann, Fibonacci,
   Pascal (triangle), Euclid(ean), Lagrange, Dirac. 布朗运动 → Brownian motion.
