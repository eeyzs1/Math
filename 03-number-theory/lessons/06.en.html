Number Theory / LESSON 06

The Chinese Remainder Theorem

Goal for this lesson: explain "The Chinese Remainder Theorem" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers, algebra, and proofs. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

When m and n are coprime, the system x≡a (mod m), x≡b (mod n) has exactly one solution mod mn. Construct it by substituting one modulus at a time: write x=a+mt, substitute into the second congruence, and solve for t mod n ("dividing by the coefficient" is really multiplying by a modular inverse — see the Bézout method in the previous lesson). Structurally this is the ring isomorphism Z/mn≅Z/m×Z/n: a problem modulo a large modulus splits into problems modulo small moduli.

A Derivation, Step by Step

x≡2 (mod 3), x≡3 (mod 5): x=2+3t ⇒ 2+3t≡3 (mod 5) ⇒ 3t≡1 ⇒ t≡2 (mod 5), giving x=8. Check: 8≡2 ✓ and 8≡3 ✓.

Pause and Try It

Find the smallest positive integer satisfying both x≡1 (mod 2) and x≡2 (mod 3).

Show the solution and self-check criteria

x=1+2t ⇒ 1+2t≡2 (mod 3) ⇒ 2t≡1 ⇒ t≡2 (mod 3), so x=1+4=5. Indeed 5 is odd and leaves remainder 2 on division by 3; the solution is unique mod 6.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
