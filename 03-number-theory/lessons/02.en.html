Number Theory / LESSON 02

Primes and Unique Factorization

Goal for this lesson: explain "Primes and Unique Factorization" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers, algebra, and proofs. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A prime is an integer greater than 1 whose only divisors are 1 and itself. The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: every integer greater than 1 can be written as a product of primes, and uniquely so up to the order of the factors. There are infinitely many primes: if there were only finitely many, multiply them all together and add 1; the new number is divisible by none of the known primes — a contradiction.

A Derivation, Step by Step

360=2³×3²×5. Computing a gcd from factorizations: 48=2⁴×3 and 18=2×3²; take each common prime to the lower power, giving gcd=2×3=6, in agreement with the Euclidean algorithm — unique factorization is what lets us talk about "common divisors" unambiguously.

Pause and Try It

Write out the prime factorizations of 90 and 84, read off the gcd, and check it with the Euclidean algorithm.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

90=2×3²×5 and 84=2²×3×7, so gcd=2×3=6. Euclid: 90=84+6 and 84=14×6 with remainder 0, so gcd=6. They agree because both methods are counting the same common prime factors.
