Number Theory / LESSON 03

Congruence: Looking Only at Remainders

Goal for this lesson: explain "Congruence" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Integers, algebra, and proofs. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

a≡b (mod m) means that m divides a−b, where m is a positive integer. Congruences are well suited to clocks and periodic behavior; they can be added and multiplied, but cancellation demands conditions. Congruence classes are the standard example in the lesson on equivalence relations and quotient sets in 01: Z/m is precisely a quotient set.

A Derivation, Step by Step

17≡5 (mod 12), so 17 hours from now and 5 hours from now land on the same clock position. Modulo 6, 2×1≡2×4, yet one may not divide by 2 and conclude 1≡4.

Pause and Try It

Find the remainder when the 100th power of 7 is divided by 10.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The units digits of 7¹,7²,7³,7⁴ are 7,9,3,1 in turn, repeating every 4 steps. Since 100 is a multiple of 4, the remainder is 1.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
