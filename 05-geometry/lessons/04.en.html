Geometry / LESSON 04

Conic Sections

Goal for this lesson: explain "Conic Sections" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra, coordinates, and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The set of points whose distances to two fixed points (the foci) sum to a constant 2a (which must exceed the distance between the foci) is an ellipse; the set where the absolute difference of the distances is a constant 2a (smaller than the distance between the foci) is a hyperbola; the set of points equidistant from a focus and a directrix is a parabola — algebraically, all are quadratic curves. Eccentricity is defined uniformly as 'distance to the focus over distance to the directrix'; for central conics it is exactly e=c/a: e<1 ellipse, e=1 parabola (immediate from the definition), e>1 hyperbola, with the circle as the special case e=0.

A Derivation, Step by Step

x²/9+y²/4=1: a=3, b=2, c=√(9−4)=√5; the foci are (±√5,0) and the eccentricity is √5/3<1 — an ellipse.

Pause and Try It

Identify the curve x²/4−y²/4=1 and find its asymptotes.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

A hyperbola (a difference of squares). Here a=b=2 and the asymptotes are y=±x: x²−y²=(x+y)(x−y)=4, so as |x| grows the curve hugs the two lines. The eccentricity √8/2=√2>1 agrees with the classification.
