How Distance Becomes an Equation

Goal for this lesson: explain "How Distance Becomes an Equation" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra, coordinates, and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The Euclidean distance between two points in the plane comes from the Pythagorean theorem: d²=(x₂−x₁)²+(y₂−y₁)². Coordinates translate constraints about shapes into algebra.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The points at distance r from the origin satisfy x²+y²=r² — this is the circle. Moving the center to (a,b) changes the equation to (x−a)²+(y−b)²=r².

Pause and Try It

Write the equation of the circle with center (2,−1) and radius 3, and decide whether (5,−1) lies on it.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

(x−2)²+(y+1)²=9. Substituting (5,−1) gives 9+0=9, so it lies on the circle.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
