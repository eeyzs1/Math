Solving Triangles: The Laws of Sines and Cosines

Goal for this lesson: explain "Solving Triangles" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra, coordinates, and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The law of cosines c²=a²+b²−2ab·cosC extends the Pythagorean theorem to arbitrary angles (at C=90° it reduces to it); the law of sines reads a/sinA=b/sinB=c/sinC=2R, where R is the circumradius. Three sides (SSS), two sides with the included angle (SAS), and two angles with a side (AAS) can all be solved.

A Derivation, Step by Step

a=5, b=7, C=60°: c²=25+49−2×35×0.5=39, so c=√39≈6.24. Then cosA=(49+39−25)/(2×7×6.24)=63/87.4≈0.72, giving A≈44°. Law-of-sines check: 5/sin44°≈7.2≈6.24/sin60°.

Pause and Try It

Ranging across a river: with a 100 m baseline, the lines from its two ends to a target on the far bank make angles of 65° and 80° with the baseline. How far is the target from the nearer end?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The third angle is 35°. By the law of sines, d/sin65°=100/sin35°, so d≈100×0.906/0.574≈158 m. A short baseline plus two angles amplifies into a long distance — a triangle turns local measurements into global information.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
