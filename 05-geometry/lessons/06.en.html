Geometry / LESSON 06

Circles and Angles: The Inscribed Angle Theorem

Goal for this lesson: explain "Circles and Angles" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra, coordinates, and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

An angle whose vertex lies on a circle and whose sides meet the circle again at two points is an inscribed angle. Inscribed angle theorem: inscribed angles subtending the same arc are equal, each being half the central angle of that arc. Corollaries: the inscribed angle over a diameter is a right angle; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

A Derivation, Step by Step

If AB is a diameter and C lies on the circle, then ∠ACB=90°. Conversely: the midpoint of the hypotenuse of a right triangle is equidistant from all three vertices — the theorem that the median to the hypotenuse equals half the hypotenuse is essentially the statement 'the circumcenter sits at the midpoint of the hypotenuse'.

Pause and Try It

Choose C,D on the same arc on one side of chord AB, distinct from A,B. Why is ∠ACB=∠ADB? What if C,D lie on opposite sides of the chord? What is the locus of points subtending a fixed angle θ with 0<θ<π at segment AB?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

If C,D are on the same side of chord AB, the angles intercept the same arc not containing their vertices, so they are equal. On opposite sides, their intercepted arcs form the whole circle, so the angles are supplementary rather than generally equal. For 0<θ<π, the locus consists of two arcs symmetric about AB, excluding A,B. At θ=π/2 the arcs form the circle with diameter AB, minus its endpoints.
