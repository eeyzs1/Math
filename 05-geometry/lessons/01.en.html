Geometry / LESSON 01

Similarity and Congruence: From Measuring to Reasoning

Goal for this lesson: explain "Similarity and Congruence" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra, coordinates, and basic proof. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Congruent means related by a translation, rotation, or reflection (all sides and angles matching); similar means the same shape possibly at a different scale: corresponding angles equal and corresponding sides proportional. AA (two pairs of equal angles) is enough to conclude that two triangles are similar. Similarity turns 'looks proportional' into a provable conclusion — the Pythagorean theorem itself can be derived from similarity.

A Derivation, Step by Step

The altitude to the hypotenuse of a right triangle splits it into two smaller triangles, each similar to the original (they share an acute angle and both have a right angle — AA). Proportions among corresponding sides give a²=c·p and b²=c·q (p and q are the two segments the altitude cuts on the hypotenuse); adding them yields a²+b²=c².

Pause and Try It

The altitude to the hypotenuse of a 3-4-5 right triangle splits the hypotenuse into which two segments? How long is the altitude?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

With c=5, the segments are a²/c=9/5 and b²/c=16/5, which sum to exactly 5; the altitude satisfies h²=(9/5)(16/5), so h=12/5=2.4. Everything follows from ratios coming from similarity — no measuring needed.
