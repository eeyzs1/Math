Linear Algebra / LESSON 05

Linear Independence, Bases, and Dimension

Goal for this lesson: explain "Linear Independence, Bases, and Dimension" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Elementary algebra, the coordinate plane. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A list of vectors is linearly independent when 'only the all-zero combination yields the zero vector'; a basis is a list that both spans the whole space and is independent. Key theorem: any two bases of the same space have the same number of elements, and this number is called the dimension — its well-definedness rests on the replacement lemma (an independent list cannot be longer than a spanning list).

A Derivation, Step by Step

In R², (1,0) and (0,1) form a basis; (1,1) and (2,2) do not (they are dependent). (1,0) and (1,1) also form a basis: a(1,0)+b(1,1)=(a+b,b), so taking b=y and a=x−y reaches any point, and a zero combination forces a=b=0.

Pause and Try It

Determine whether (1,1,0), (0,1,1), and (1,0,1) form a basis of R³.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Solve a(1,1,0)+b(0,1,1)+c(1,0,1)=(0,0,0): a+c=0, a+b=0, b+c=0 give a=b=c=0. The three vectors are independent and span R³, hence they form a basis. Equivalently, the determinant is 2≠0.
