Linear Algebra / LESSON 06

Kernel, Image, and the Rank–Nullity Theorem

Goal for this lesson: explain "Kernel, Image, and the Rank–Nullity Theorem" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Elementary algebra, the coordinate plane. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

For a linear map A, the kernel ker A is the set of inputs mapped to zero, and the image im A is the set of all outputs. Rank–nullity theorem: dim ker A + dim im A equals the dimension of the domain. The kernel measures 'how much information is lost', the image measures 'where the map reaches', and the two trade off against each other. The domain is assumed finite-dimensional.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Let A have both rows equal to (1,1): the kernel is the line x+y=0 (one-dimensional) and the image is the line x=y (one-dimensional); 1+1=2 matches the domain R².

Pause and Try It

What are the dimensions of the kernel and the image of the projection (x,y,z)→(x,y,0)? Verify the theorem.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The kernel is the z-axis (dimension 1) and the image is the xy-plane (dimension 2); 1+2=3. The dimensions squeezed away plus the dimensions of the output exactly equal the original dimension.
