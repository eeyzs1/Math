Linear Algebra / LESSON 01

Linear Combinations and Span

Goal for this lesson: explain "Linear Combinations and Span" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Elementary algebra, the coordinate plane. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A vector can be viewed as an arrow or as an ordered list of data. av+bw is a linear combination. The set of all linear combinations forms the span; it answers 'where can these directions take us'.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Let v=(1,0) and w=(0,1). Any (x,y)=xv+yw, so they span the plane. If instead w=(2,0), every combination stays on the horizontal axis and (0,1) cannot be reached.

Pause and Try It

Do (1,1) and (2,2) span the plane? Demonstrate with a point that cannot be reached.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

No. Every combination has the form (a+2b, a+2b) — the two coordinates are equal, so (1,0) is out of reach. The two vectors are linearly dependent; the second direction adds no new degree of freedom.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
