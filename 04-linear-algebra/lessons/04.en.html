Linear Algebra / LESSON 04

The Determinant as an Area Scale Factor

Goal for this lesson: explain "The Determinant as an Area Scale Factor" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Elementary algebra, the coordinate plane. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The two columns of a 2×2 matrix A record where the two basis vectors land. det(A)=ad−bc is the oriented area scale factor; its absolute value is the area scaling and its sign records orientation. Multiplicativity, det(AB)=det(A)det(B), further makes the determinant a group homomorphism — it served as an example in Lesson 05 of 02.

A Derivation, Step by Step

A=diag(2,3) turns the unit square into a rectangle of width 2 and height 3; the determinant is 6. If the two columns are parallel, the figure is squashed onto a line, the area is 0, and the map is not invertible.

Pause and Try It

A matrix has columns (1,2) and (2,4). Can the input be recovered uniquely from the output?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

No: the determinant is 1×4−2×2=0. The second column is twice the first, so for instance the inputs (2,0) and (0,1) produce the same output.
