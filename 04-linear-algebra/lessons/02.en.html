Linear Algebra / LESSON 02

Systems of Linear Equations and Elimination

Goal for this lesson: explain "Systems of Linear Equations and Elimination" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Elementary algebra, the coordinate plane. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A solution of a linear system is a point that satisfies all the equations at once. Elimination adds a multiple of one equation to another, leaving the solution set unchanged while peeling off variables one by one. A 2×2 system has exactly three possible fates: a unique solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions — geometrically, two lines that intersect, are parallel, or coincide.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Adding x+y=3 and 2x−y=0 gives 3x=3, so x=1 and y=2. Replace the second equation by 2x+2y=5: it contradicts twice the first, 2x+2y=6 — no solution. Replace it by 2x+2y=6 and it merely repeats the first — infinitely many solutions.

Pause and Try It

Solve 2x+y=5 and x−y=1 by elimination, and explain what uniqueness looks like geometrically and algebraically.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Adding the equations gives 3x=6, so x=2 and y=1. Geometrically: the two lines have different slopes, so they meet at exactly one point. Algebraically: after elimination the coefficient 3 of x is nonzero, so x is uniquely determined (its determinant criterion appears in Lesson 04).

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
