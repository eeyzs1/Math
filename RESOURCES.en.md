# Textbook Sources and How to Use Them

Retrieval date: 2026-11-01 (the advanced-direction addenda were verified on 2026-11-07). The items below are open textbooks and courses published directly by their authors or universities; their texts are not copied. The Chinese tutorial is an independently written introductory exposition, mirrored here in English. When reading the original material, look up chapters by topic — there is no need to grind through a graduate course from its first chapter.

## Teaching Method

- [teach SKILL.md](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/productivity/teach/SKILL.md): read in full; its designs for short lessons, immediate feedback, retrieval, and spaced review were adapted. Found via find-skills; skills.sh showed roughly 896k installs on the page at the time and the GitHub API showed roughly 308k stars for the repository at the time — the numbers are retrieval snapshots and cannot replace a review of the textbook content. Not installed globally.
- [MSC2020](https://msc2020.org/): the scope reference for the research classification; the study groupings are this project's own.

## Foundational Textbooks

- [OpenStax Precalculus 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/precalculus-2e): functions, equations, trigonometry, analytic geometry; corresponds to 00, the elementary parts of 02, and the start of 05.
- [Oscar Levin, Discrete Mathematics](https://discrete.openmathbooks.org/dmoi4.html): logic, proof, counting, graphs; corresponds to 01, the beginnings of 03, 08, and the algorithm preparation for 14.
- [Jim Hefferon, Linear Algebra](https://hefferon.net/linearalgebra/): vector spaces, matrices, determinants; corresponds to 04. The original book's full course has additional prerequisites; the two lessons here need only elementary algebra.
- [OpenStax Calculus Volume 1](https://openstax.org/details/books/calculus-volume-1): limits, derivatives, and integrals; corresponds to 06 and the foundations of 12 and 13.
- [OpenStax Introductory Statistics 2e](https://openstax.org/details/books/introductory-statistics-2e): probability, describing data, sampling, and inference; corresponds to 09 and 10.

## Advanced Directions (not beginner reading)

- [MIT 18.703 Modern Algebra](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-703-modern-algebra-spring-2013/): the group–ring–field direction of 02.
- [MIT 18.785 Number Theory I](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-785-number-theory-i-fall-2021/): the algebraic number theory direction of 03; a graduate course.
- [MIT 18.901 Introduction to Topology](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-901-introduction-to-topology-fall-2004/): continuity, connectedness, compactness in 07.
- [MIT 18.03SC Differential Equations](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-03sc-differential-equations-fall-2011/): the ordinary differential equations of 11, plus background for the oscillations of 15 and the growth models of 16.
- [MIT 18.303 Linear Partial Differential Equations](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-303-linear-partial-differential-equations-fall-2006/): the PDE directions of 11 and 15.
- [MIT 18.335J Numerical Methods](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-335j-introduction-to-numerical-methods-spring-2019/): computational error and stability in 12; graduate level.
- [MIT 6.003 Signals and Systems](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-003-signals-and-systems-fall-2011/): signals, systems, and transform background, corresponding to the applications of 14 and 15.

The number-systems and axioms lessons of 17 serve as cross-chapter literacy guides; this edition does not include a chronology with original-source scholarship. Full convex optimization for 13, complete information theory and coding for 14, and the financial mathematics of 16 still need dedicated advanced bibliographies — the foundational textbooks above do not replace sources for those advanced areas.
