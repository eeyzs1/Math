Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems / LESSON 02

Separable Equations: Pulling the Rate of Change Apart

Goal for this lesson: explain "Separable Equations" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Derivatives and integrals; Lesson 7 on the phase plane requires linear algebra (eigenvalues: see 04, Lesson 07). Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

When y′=g(x)h(y), write y′ as dy/dx and "separate": dy/h(y)=g(x)dx, then integrate both sides to solve (the step is justified by integration by substitution, see 06, Lesson 05). Exponential growth, the logistic equation, Newton's law of cooling, and radioactive decay are all instances — this is the first method that generalizes exponential growth.

A Derivation, Step by Step

y′=y(1−y), y(0)=1/2: since 1/[y(1−y)]=1/y+1/(1−y), integrating gives ln[y/(1−y)]=x+C, and solving for y yields y=1/(1+Ae⁻ˣ); the initial value gives A=1, so the S-shaped curve is y=1/(1+e⁻ˣ).

Pause and Try It

Solve Newton's law of cooling T′=−k(T−T₀); according to what law does the temperature difference evolve? (Used directly in the physics lessons of 15 Mathematical Physics.)

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Integrating dT/(T−T₀)=−k dt gives ln(T−T₀)=−kt+C, hence T=T₀+(T(0)−T₀)e^(−kt): the temperature difference decays exponentially. "Separation of variables" puts each of the two entangled variables on its own side so that each can be integrated separately.
