Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems / LESSON 09

Difference Equations and the Logistic Map

Goal for this lesson: explain "Difference Equations and the Logistic Map" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Derivatives and integrals; Lesson 7 on the phase plane requires linear algebra (eigenvalues: see 04, Lesson 07). Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A discrete-time system xₙ₊₁=f(xₙ) evolves by iteration. A fixed point satisfies x*=f(x*), and |f′(x*)|<1 is sufficient for local attraction. The logistic map xₙ₊₁=rxₙ(1−xₙ) is usually studied for 0≤r≤4 and 0≤x₀≤1. For interior initial values, 0<r≤1 leads to 0, and 1<r<3 to (r−1)/r; endpoints 0 and 1 need separate treatment. Period-doubling begins at r=3, with an accumulation point near 3.56995. Beyond it, chaotic regimes coexist with periodic windows; not every r>3.57 is chaotic.

A Derivation, Step by Step

r=2, x₀=0.3: 0.3→0.42→0.4872→0.4997→… converging to 0.5=(r−1)/r; at that point f′=2(1−2×0.5)=0, so it is superstable. With the same r and x₀=0.9: 0.9→0.18→0.2952→0.4161→… still converges to 0.5.

Pause and Try It

For r=3.2, is the fixed point (r−1)/r=0.6875 stable? What is the long-run behavior likely to be?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

|f′|=|3.2×(1−2×0.6875)|=1.2>1, so the fixed point loses stability; the orbit approaches a cycle of period 2 (bouncing back and forth between two values). That period-2 cycle destabilizes into period 4 at r≈3.45, then 8, 16, … — period-doubling on the road to chaos. Even a deterministic system can "look random".
