Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems / LESSON 10

Ergodicity: Time Averages Equal Space Averages

Goal for this lesson: explain "Ergodicity" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Derivatives and integrals; Lesson 7 on the phase plane requires linear algebra (eigenvalues: see 04, Lesson 07). Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A system is ergodic if the long-time average along almost every orbit equals the average taken with respect to the invariant distribution: lim(1/T)∫₀ᵀ g(x(t))dt=∫g dμ. The physical picture: the long-run experience of one molecule in a cup of coffee ≈ a statistical snapshot of all the molecules — follow one orbit long enough, and there is no need to sweep the whole space. Ergodicity is weaker than mixing: ergodicity guarantees only that the averages agree, while mixing further demands that the system gradually forget its initial value.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Rotation of the circle x↦x+α (mod 1): when α is irrational the orbit is uniformly dense (equidistribution), the time average equals the average over the circle, and the system is ergodic; when α=p/q is rational the orbit consists of only q points, and the system is not ergodic. The Markov chain motto of 09 — "any initial value → the stationary distribution" — is precisely the discrete version of ergodicity.

Pause and Try It

For an irrational rotation, why does the time average along a single orbit equal the average over the circle? What are the exceptions in "almost every orbit"?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The orbit spreads over the circle uniformly: sampling any continuous function along the orbit is equivalent to sampling from the uniform distribution (Weyl's equidistribution theorem). The exceptional set has measure zero (for example, periodic orbits starting from rational points) — the ergodic theorem holds for "almost all" initial values, and a few exceptions do not spoil the statistical conclusion.
