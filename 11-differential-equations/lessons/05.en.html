Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems / LESSON 05

First-Order Linear Equations and Integrating Factors

Goal for this lesson: explain "First-Order Linear Equations and Integrating Factors" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Derivatives and integrals; Lesson 7 on the phase plane requires linear algebra (eigenvalues: see 04, Lesson 07). Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Multiply the standard form y′+p(t)y=q(t) on both sides by μ(t)=e^(∫p dt) and the left-hand side becomes exactly (μy)′, so a single integration solves the equation. This is the technique of "completing an incomplete derivative"; the choice of μ is dictated entirely by p.

A Derivation, Step by Step

y′+2y=e^(−t): μ=e^(2t), so (e^(2t)y)′=e^t; integrating gives e^(2t)y=e^t+C, hence y=e^(−t)+Ce^(−2t).

Pause and Try It

Solve y′+y=t with initial value y(0)=1.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

μ=e^t, so (e^t y)′=te^t; integration by parts (see 06, Lesson 05) gives e^t y=(t−1)e^t+C, hence y=t−1+Ce^(−t). Substituting y(0)=1 gives C=2. Check: y′+y=(1−2e^(−t))+(t−1+2e^(−t))=t ✓.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
