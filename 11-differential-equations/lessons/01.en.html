Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems / LESSON 01

From Laws of Change to Exponential Growth

Goal for this lesson: explain "From Laws of Change to Exponential Growth" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Derivatives and integrals; Lesson 7 on the phase plane requires linear algebra (eigenvalues: see 04, Lesson 07). Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

y′=ky says that the rate of change is proportional to the current amount. Given the initial value y(0)=y₀, the solution is y=y₀eᵏᵗ. The equation describes the law; the initial value selects the particular trajectory.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Take k=0.1 and y₀=100; then y(t)=100e⁰·¹ᵗ. To verify, differentiate to get 10e⁰·¹ᵗ, which is exactly 0.1y (the differentiation rule for the exponential function: see 06, Lesson 03), and check that the value at t=0 is 100.

Pause and Try It

What is the solution of y′=−2y, y(0)=3? What happens to it in the long run?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

y=3e⁻²ᵗ. As t tends to positive infinity it tends to 0, yet at every finite time it is still positive. The model may be a poor fit for a small number of discrete individuals.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
