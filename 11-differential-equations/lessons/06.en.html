Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems / LESSON 06

Second-Order Equations with Constant Coefficients

Goal for this lesson: explain "Second-Order Equations with Constant Coefficients" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Derivatives and integrals; Lesson 7 on the phase plane requires linear algebra (eigenvalues: see 04, Lesson 07). Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Trying y=e^(rt) in y″+by′+cy=0 leads to the characteristic equation r²+br+c=0. Two distinct real roots give a combination of exponentials; a repeated root gives (c₁+c₂t)e^(rt) (the extra factor of t is what yields two independent solutions); complex roots α±βi give e^(αt)(c₁cosβt+c₂sinβt) (by Euler's formula e^(iθ)=cosθ+i·sinθ, which follows from the series expansions in the 06 lesson on series; for the geometry of eigenvalues, see 04, Lesson 07). The three damping regimes of the spring equation correspond exactly to the three kinds of roots.

A Derivation, Step by Step

y″−y=0: r=±1, so y=c₁e^t+c₂e^(−t). y″+4y=0: r=±2i, so y=c₁cos2t+c₂sin2t, in agreement with the lesson on simple harmonic oscillation.

Pause and Try It

What is the general solution of y″+2y′+y=0? What damping regime is this?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

r²+2r+1=(r+1)² is a repeated root, so y=(c₁+c₂t)e^(−t). This is critical damping: with slightly less damping the roots become complex and the solution starts to oscillate; with more, it decays slowly as a pure exponential. The repeated root is exactly the boundary between the two regimes.
