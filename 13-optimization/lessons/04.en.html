Linear Programming and Vertices

Goal for this lesson: explain "Linear Programming and Vertices" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; later lessons require calculus and linear algebra. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

When the objective and all constraints are linear, the problem is a linear program, in standard form: optimize cᵀx subject to Ax≤b, x≥0; the feasible region is a convex polyhedron. Basic fact: a bounded optimum is attained at a vertex — so 'searching over a continuum' reduces to 'comparing finitely many vertices', which is where the simplex method comes from.

A Derivation, Step by Step

max x+y subject to x+2y≤4, x≥0, y≥0: the feasible region is a triangle with vertices (0,0), (4,0), (0,2) and objective values 0, 4, 2 — the optimum sits at (4,0).

Pause and Try It

Add the constraint x≤3 to the previous problem. How do the optimum and its value change?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The feasible region becomes the quadrilateral (0,0), (3,0), (3,0.5), (0,2) with objective values 0, 3, 3.5, 2; the optimum moves to the new vertex (3,0.5). The optimum is still attained at a vertex — slice off one corner, and the optimum simply relocates to the new corner.
