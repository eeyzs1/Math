Convex Sets and Convex Functions

Goal for this lesson: explain "Convex Sets and Convex Functions" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Algebra; later lessons require calculus and linear algebra. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A convex set contains the line segment between any two of its points. A function on a convex domain is convex if f(λx+(1−λ)y)≤λf(x)+(1−λ)f(y) for 0≤λ≤1. A convex objective over a convex feasible set makes every local minimum global. Convexity is an important sufficient structure, not the only possible class with that property.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Both x² and eˣ are convex. For x³ the second derivative is 6x, so it is concave on the negative half-line and convex on the positive half-line, with an inflection at 0; it is not globally convex. Check derivative signs rather than relying only on a sketch.

Pause and Try It

Prove that a local minimum of a convex function must be a global minimum.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Suppose x* is a local minimum yet some y has f(y)<f(x*). Choose a small λ so that z=λy+(1−λ)x* lies in the neighborhood of x*; convexity gives f(z)≤λf(y)+(1−λ)f(x*)<f(x*), contradicting local minimality. Convexity pulls 'better far away' into 'better nearby'.
