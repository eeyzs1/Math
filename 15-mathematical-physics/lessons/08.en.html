Coulomb's Law and the Inverse Square

Goal for this lesson: explain "Coulomb's Law and the Inverse Square" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The force between two point charges is F=k q₁q₂/r², directed along the line joining them (like charges repel, unlike attract), structurally identical to universal gravitation. The inverse-square behavior is a geometric outcome: as the field crosses a sphere, the same amount is spread over 4πr². In the language of fields: a charge assigns an electric field E to each point, and a test charge feels the force qE; problems with multiple charges are linearized by the superposition principle — isomorphic to 04 Linear Algebra.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Electron and proton: the ratio of electrostatic force to gravity is ≈ ke²/(Gmₚmₑ)≈10³⁹ — electricity overwhelms gravity, which is why matter is neutral as a whole. Two 1-coulomb opposite charges 1 meter apart attract with F=k·1·1/1²=9×10⁹ newtons — charges of macroscopic size reveal astonishing forces.

Pause and Try It

Three equal charges sit at the vertices of an equilateral triangle: outline the reasoning for the force on a test charge at the center.

Show the solution and self-check criteria

Each vertex contributes a force vector along the joining line, of magnitude kq/r²; the three vectors are symmetrically placed, so first fix the direction of the resultant by symmetry (along the axis of symmetry), then sum components. The superposition principle = linearity: compute each contribution separately, then add the vectors — the same logic as solving a system of linear equations.
