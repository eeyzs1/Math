Mathematical Physics / LESSON 05

The One-Dimensional Wave Equation

Goal for this lesson: explain "The One-Dimensional Wave Equation" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Small transverse vibrations of a string lead to ∂²u/∂t²=c²∂²u/∂x², with c=√(tension/linear density); u(x,t) is a function of two variables — for partial derivatives and the chain rule in two variables, see 06, Lesson 09. The general solution is u=f(x−ct)+g(x+ct): any shape propagates in both directions at speed c, and solutions may be superposed — the mathematical definition of a "wave".

A Derivation, Step by Step

An initial displacement f(x) released from rest evolves into u(x,t)=½[f(x−ct)+f(x+ct)]: the initial shape splits into two halves, each propagating to one side at speed c (the d'Alembert solution).

Pause and Try It

Why does "any waveform propagates while keeping its shape" correspond exactly to this equation?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

For u=f(x−ct): u_tt=c²f″ and u_xx=f″, so u_tt=c²u_xx holds automatically, and likewise for g(x+ct); the equation is linear, so the sum of the two branches is again a solution. Conversely, the general solution of this equation is exactly the sum of two traveling waves — linear structure + second derivatives = shape-preserving propagation in both directions.
