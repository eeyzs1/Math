Mathematical Physics / LESSON 02

Newton's Second Law and Simple Harmonic Oscillation

Goal for this lesson: explain "Newton's Second Law and Simple Harmonic Oscillation" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

Newton's second law F=ma is the second-order equation m x″=F — precisely the standard object of the "second-order equations with constant coefficients" in 11 (characteristic roots ±iω). A linear spring F=−kx gives x″=−(k/m)x, with general solution x(t)=A cos(ωt)+B sin(ωt), ω=√(k/m). The amplitude is set by the initial conditions, while the period 2π√(m/k) is independent of the amplitude (isochronism).

A Derivation, Step by Step

With m=1, k=4, x(0)=1, x′(0)=0: x=cos 2t, with period 2π/2=π. Verify that x″=−4cos 2t=−4x. Doubling the initial values gives 2cos 2t, still oscillating with the same period.

Pause and Try It

If k and m are both multiplied by 4, how does the period change? And if only m is multiplied by 4?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

T=2π√(m/k). Multiplying both by 4 leaves the ratio, and hence the period, unchanged; increasing m alone slows the motion by a factor of 2. The dimensional analysis lesson already anticipated that the period must be √(m/k) times a constant.
