Mathematical Physics / LESSON 03

Conserved Quantities Connect the Local and the Global

Goal for this lesson: explain "Conserved Quantities Connect the Local and the Global" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

An undamped spring satisfies m x″=−kx. The sum of kinetic and potential energy, E=mx′²/2+kx²/2, is a conserved quantity. Here m and k are assumed to be positive constants, with no external forces.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Differentiating E gives E′=mx′x″+kxx′=x′(mx″+kx)=0. The equation of motion makes the bracket vanish, so the energy never changes. Conservation is derived from the law of motion, not posited as an extra guess.

Pause and Try It

Once the damping term c x′ (c>0) is added, what is the rate of change of the energy?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

The equation is mx″+cx′+kx=0; substituting gives E′=−cx′²≤0. Mechanical energy dissipates, so the undamped conservation conclusion no longer carries over.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
