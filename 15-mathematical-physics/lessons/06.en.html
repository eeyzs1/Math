Newton's Law of Cooling: The Physics of a First-Order Equation

Goal for this lesson: explain "Newton's Law of Cooling" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

The rate of change of an object's temperature is proportional to its difference from the surroundings: T′=−k(T−T₀). It is a direct physical instance of the separable and first-order linear equations of 11 (explicit solution T=T₀+(T(0)−T₀)e^(−kt)); heat exchange among several objects becomes a system of equations — the prototype of a thermal network.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Coffee at 85°C sits in a 25°C room, with k=0.1/minute: T=25+60e^(−0.1t). After 30 minutes T=25+60e⁻³≈28°C; the temperature difference halves every ln2/k≈6.9 minutes.

Pause and Try It

How many half-lives does it take to reduce the temperature difference to 1% of its initial value? Does it depend on the initial difference?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

1%=2⁻ⁿ gives n=log₂100≈6.6 half-lives, independent of the initial value — exponential decay cares only about proportions. Conversely, using the cooling law to infer the "time of death" (given normal body temperature and the ambient temperature) is a standard application in forensics: measure k, measure the current temperature, and solve for t.
