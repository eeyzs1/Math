Potential Energy and the Energy Method

Goal for this lesson: explain "Potential Energy and the Energy Method" in your own words, then complete the transfer exercise below on your own.

Prerequisites: Calculus, linear algebra, and differential equations. Try the worked example first; if the notation feels heavy, go back to the preparation module.

Intuition First, Then Symbols

A conservative force satisfies F(x)=−V′(x) (the spring's F=−kx corresponds to V=kx²/2), and the work done depends only on the endpoints: W=−ΔV. The energy method yields qualitative conclusions without solving the equation: conservation of the total energy E=½mv²+V confines the motion to the region V≤E, with turning points given by V(x)=E.

A Derivation, Step by Step

Pull the spring to x₀ and release it: E=½kx₀²; at position x the speed satisfies ½mv²=½k(x₀²−x²) — v(x) is obtained directly, without solving the second-order equation.

Pause and Try It

What can the energy method tell you for the potential energy V(x)=x⁴/4? What if no explicit solution can be found?

Show the solution and self-check criteria

E=½mv²+x⁴/4 is conserved (below we take m=1 and write ½v²+x⁴/4): the motion is confined to |x|≤(4E)^(1/4), the speed vanishes at the endpoints and reverses, and the orbit is symmetric and periodic. The period integral need not be elementary, but the qualitative conclusions (accessible region, turning points, symmetry) do not depend on an explicit solution — such is the value of qualitative analysis.

Self-check: Is the conclusion correct? Are the conditions stated? Can you explain the key steps without looking at the example? If you only recalled the answer, redo the lesson.
